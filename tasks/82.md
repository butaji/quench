# 82: Choose the heap and collector on the Score × RSS front

## Outcome

Allocation is a pointer bump on the common path. Most objects die young and
are reclaimed without being traced individually. The heap's peak size is
the live set plus a headroom chosen on the Score × RSS Pareto front, and
freed pages go back to the operating system.

## Current gap

The heap is a non-moving mark-sweep over every slot, triggered by allocation
count with a fixed floor of 384, and each payload is a separate `malloc`
(the 2026-09-23 review). v2 evaluated a young-space allocator (8), packed mark
bits (23), survival-driven cadence (54, 64), malloc zone policy (45, 173,
353, 356), and flat-string buffer reuse (238). It proposed sweeping by bitmap
word (278), address-ordered free lists (279), a corpus-derived trigger (280),
page return (285, 298), mmap reservation (286), and write-barrier elision
(250). None of the proposals was measured, and task 77's allocation row
reduced all of them to a single candidate.

## Scope and implementation sequence

1. Model the collector as a named state machine (idle, marking, sweeping,
   with lazy sweeping steps). The trigger is live bytes against a headroom
   factor, not a count of allocations. Choose the factor and the nursery size
   from task 77's host facts and the paired gate, never from one axis.
2. Spike a bump-pointer nursery with copy-out promotion (generation
   scavenging). The slab indirection of `Value` lets cells move without
   rewriting references. Add a write barrier with a remembered set, and elide
   it statically where the binding-time analysis proves the target is young or the value is not
   a heap reference.
3. Spike mark-region allocation (Immix): bump allocation into free lines of
   partly used blocks, with opportunistic evacuation. Compare it with the
   nursery spike, and keep one design or their composition.
4. Pretenure by allocation site: sites whose objects mostly survive allocate
   directly in the old space. The decision is a monotone per-site state
   (README), so it cannot oscillate.
5. Allocate payloads (property arenas, elements, strings) in the same managed
   space as their cells, not in individual `malloc` blocks. Fold allocations
   that the residual makes together into one bump.
6. Reserve the heap's address range with `mmap` at the host page size
   recorded in task 77's host facts.
   Sweep by mark-bitmap word, and return fully free pages with
   `madvise(MADV_FREE_REUSABLE)` after a sweep crosses a named threshold. Keep
   v2's malloc zone policy for what remains in `malloc`.
7. Evaluate immediate reclamation by reference counting for objects that
   die acyclic and young, combined with tracing for cycles (QuickJS; RC
   Immix). It is the main reason QuickJS's peak RSS stays close to its live
   set. Record large pages for the arena as unavailable to macOS user
   processes unless the platform exposes them.
8. Evaluate concurrent sweeping on a second core. It improves wall-clock
   Score and does not change semantics, because sweeping never runs
   finalizers or guest code.

## Verification

GC stress modes (collect at every safepoint, at every allocation) across the
full conformance ratchet. WeakRef, FinalizationRegistry, and ephemeron tests
under every retained design. Barrier-omission detection: a debug build checks
the remembered-set invariant at every collection.

## Definition of done

- Allocation of a small cell on the common path is a bump and a bounds check.
- The trigger, headroom, nursery size, and pretenuring states are declared
  data, and each value comes from the paired gate.
- Freed pages return to the OS, and peak RSS tracks the live set plus the
  declared headroom.

## Performance evidence

Report GC time share, pause distribution, promotion rate, pretenured-site
share, peak heap versus live set, and pages returned per fixture, plus the
paired gate. Splay and EarleyBoyer are the primary fixtures.
